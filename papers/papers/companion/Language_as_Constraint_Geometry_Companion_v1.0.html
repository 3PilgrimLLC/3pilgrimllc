1. Why This Paper Exists

For centuries, human language was understood as an open domain shaped by culture, accident, and historical drift. But once every attested language can be embedded into a shared space—mathematically, geometrically, or through modern alignment techniques—the apparent diversity collapses. Languages cluster into a small number of recurrent structural regimes that no cultural theory can fully explain. This paper exists to supply the missing reduction: a systems‑theoretic account showing that when a communication system is pre‑literate, tokenized, memory‑bounded, and forced to maintain fidelity across generations, only three deep structural solutions remain dynamically stable.

2. What the Paper Says

The core claim is simple:

When a tokenized biological channel operates under three universal constraints—medium degradation, working‑memory limits, and intergenerational fidelity—its structural space collapses to three and only three stable redundancy geometries:

1. Rhythmic‑Segmental: redundancy paid in discrete temporal pulses.

2. Harmonic‑Contour: redundancy paid in smooth continuous curvature across sequences.

3. Templatic‑Positional: redundancy paid in rigid positional frames with high internal variability.

These geometries appear across human oral traditions, non‑human signaling (cetaceans, primates, insects), and even biochemical information systems. The paper formalizes the constraint manifold producing these attractors and validates the model using four undeciphered or minimally attested ancient corpora whose structural signatures match the predicted basins.

3. What Distinguishes This Framework from Existing Approaches

Unlike typology, historical linguistics, or cultural diffusion models, this framework is not genealogical and not historical. It does not classify languages by family tree, region, or cultural ancestry.

It identifies a physical design space —a constraint polytope—generated by noise, memory, and fidelity requirements. Within that polytope, only three stable minima exist.

Where existing work treats diversity as a product of culture, this model treats it as ornamentation atop deep, universal redundancy geometries forced by biology and physics.

4. Theoretical Implications (If correct)

If the framework is correct, much of linguistic diversity becomes secondary structure rather than primary architecture.

It implies:

- All pre‑literate systems must fall into one of three attractors.

- Deep grammar is determined by redundancy strategy, not cultural invention.

- Genealogical evolution occurs within a basin, not across structural space.

- Transitional corpora should preserve basin signatures even without semantics.

- Non‑human systems should exhibit homologous redundancy patterns.

This model turns the question “Why do languages vary?” into “Which redundancy budget do they choose under constraint?”

5. Potential Implications

Not predictions—just possible downstream uses:

- A null hypothesis for decipherment when no bilinguals exist.

- A general substrate for modeling proto‑communication systems.

- A framework for comparing human and non‑human signaling architectures.

- Methods for identifying when literacy temporarily masks deep structure.

- Constraint‑based priors for computational reconstruction of ancient scripts.

6. Position in the Five‑Paper Series

This is Paper 2 in a five‑part sequence on how constrained systems acquire stable structure across time.

This entry provides the pre‑semantic, biological communication layer: the deep redundancy geometries from which human language inherits its permissible forms.

Paper 1 (CLP) focuses on cognitive calibration through linguistic structure.

Other papers address threat‑resolution heuristics, symbolic transmission in low‑information environments, and the generalized emergence mechanism tying them together.
